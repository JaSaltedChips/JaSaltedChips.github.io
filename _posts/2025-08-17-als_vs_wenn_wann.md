Question: **"Die wurden wohl geliefert, als ich im Urlaub war."** Can I not use "wann" instead of "als"?

---
Good question — this is one of those subtle German points where **„als“ ≠ „wann“**. Let’s break it down with your sentence:

### Your sentence

**„Die wurden wohl geliefert, als ich im Urlaub war.“**
\= *They were probably delivered when I was on vacation.*

Here **„als“** is correct because you’re talking about **one specific time in the past** (during your vacation).

---

### Rule of thumb

1. **als** → one specific event or period in the past

   * *Als ich klein war, habe ich in Indien gelebt.* = When I was small, I lived in India.
   * *Es hat geregnet, als wir angekommen sind.* = It rained when we arrived.

2. **wenn** → whenever, repeated/conditional situations (can be present, past, or future)

   * *Wenn ich Kaffee trinke, kann ich nicht schlafen.* = Whenever I drink coffee, I can’t sleep.
   * *Früher, wenn ich krank war, hat meine Mutter Suppe gemacht.* = Back then, whenever I was sick, my mother made soup.

3. **wann** → used only in **questions** (direct or indirect) about *at what time*

   * *Wann kommst du nach Hause?* = When are you coming home?
   * *Weißt du, wann er ankommt?* = Do you know when he arrives?

---

### Why *„wann“* doesn’t work in your sentence

If you said:
**„Die wurden wohl geliefert, wann ich im Urlaub war.“**
that would literally mean: *“They were probably delivered, \[the time] when I was on vacation.”*

Grammatically, it sounds wrong in German because **„wann“** doesn’t introduce time clauses as conjunctions — it’s reserved for questions.

So here, only **„als“** fits.

---

👉 Short answer: No, you cannot use *„wann“* there. You need *„als“* because it’s about **a specific past timeframe**.
